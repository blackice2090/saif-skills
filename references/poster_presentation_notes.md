# Poster Presentation Notes — Source and Classification

This reference summarizes the user-provided material titled **“Scientific Poster Presentation Skills: From Preparation to Impactful Presence.”** It is a coaching source. It does not replace current SAIF official documents.

## Source classification

### OFFICIAL REQUIREMENT / OFFICIAL GUIDANCE

The official SAIF participant page currently says to:

- make the poster clear and easy to read using the approved template, presenting the problem, methodology, innovation, results, and impact;
- support project claims with evidence and experiments and keep presentation, poster, and project-file information consistent;
- coordinate team roles at the booth and stay ready to present;
- present the project in a clear sequence within **7 minutes**, covering the problem, solution, implementation and testing, and results and expected impact; prepare to answer judge questions afterward.

Source: [SAIF participant page](https://saifair.sa/participants), checked 2026-11-07. Recheck the page and downloadable participant guide before planning a live presentation because official instructions can change. The current page does not state a fixed Q&A duration.

### EXPERT GUIDANCE

The supplied training-session material recommends:

- help a passerby understand quickly why the project is worth stopping for and what result can be discussed;
- give the title and main result clear visual priority, then use method, data, metrics, comparison, and limitations to support deeper discussion;
- test the poster with someone unfamiliar with the project and ask what first attracted their attention, what they understood, and whether they found the result and method;
- make claims specific to the actual test, data, metric, and comparison; explain false positives or other relevant tradeoffs;
- trim repetition while preserving information needed to understand the problem, contribution, method, result, limitations, and next step;
- use a short greeting and opening, then invite the visitor to choose a deeper topic rather than reading the poster aloud;
- respond to the visitor's questions to determine whether they want technical, application, or impact details, without assuming their interests from appearance or title;
- stand beside the poster, keep visuals visible, point to the relevant element, maintain natural eye contact, listen, and pause after answering;
- coordinate speaker handoffs, ensure each team member understands the full project and their own contribution, and rotate roles when useful;
- answer directly, explain the supporting evidence, acknowledge relevant limits, and state what remains untested;
- rehearse aloud and compare the poster, registration information, and spoken claims for consistency.

### PRACTICAL COACHING

The following are reusable coaching exercises, not official judging criteria:

- **Three-distance test:** from a distance, identify why to stop; after approaching, identify what was built or found; in discussion, identify the evidence and limitation.
- **Unfamiliar-listener check:** after a practice run, ask the listener to restate the problem, contribution, main result, and remaining question.
- **Evidence-to-claim check:** for every number spoken, record its source data, metric, comparison, and interpretation limit.
- **Short conversation practice:** rehearse a concise opening, ask what the listener wants to explore, answer, then pause.
- **Team handoff practice:** rehearse each transition and make individual contributions clear.
- **Timing practice:** rehearse to the currently verified official limit; label any suggested section split as coaching advice, not official scoring weights.
