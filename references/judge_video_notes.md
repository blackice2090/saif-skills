# SAIF Judge / Expert Video Notes — Practical Evaluation Lens

Source: `Video Project 3.srt`, a participant guidance session by Dr. Mahmoud. This is practical expert guidance, not an official weighted judging rubric.

## 1. What the poster signals about the participant
Around 03:40–07:10, the speaker emphasizes that a strong scientific poster demonstrates:
- clarity with accuracy;
- logical sequencing;
- simplicity and intelligent concision;
- preservation of strengths and originality despite brevity;
- a complete scientific story;
- visual balance and attractiveness;
- compliance with the competition's technical poster standards.

He also stresses that the poster is the only mandatory submission item containing substantial project detail, so it is a major communication artifact for judges.

## 2. Title
Around 07:54–12:30:
- Treat the title as an independent research identity for the project.
- Avoid generic titles.
- Include the most distinguishing information when useful: objective, originality, scope, target group, methodology/tool, study variables or sample.
- The title should help a specialist immediately understand where the project's strength lies.
- Avoid unexplained abbreviations in the title.

## 3. Introduction
Around 13:04–18:55:
Use a funnel structure:
1. Broad context / why the field matters.
2. Existing knowledge, products, or prior solutions.
3. The unresolved gap or concrete problem.
4. The project's specific originality / response to that gap.
5. Clear, specific objectives.

Common failures highlighted:
- staying too general;
- never narrowing to a precise gap;
- hiding novelty inside vague prose rather than stating it clearly;
- using floating / non-specific objectives.

The last part of the introduction should logically lead into the methodology.

## 4. Methodology
Around 18:55–22:53:
A specialist should be able to understand and, in principle, reproduce the work. Include what applies:
- study / experimental design;
- materials, tools, hardware, software, or datasets;
- execution steps in chronological order;
- why the chosen method/tool/algorithm was selected instead of alternatives;
- data sources and collection methods;
- data processing and analysis;
- accuracy / error-reduction methods;
- statistics where relevant;
- protocols and procedures.

Poster-specific guidance:
- the poster is visual, so do not turn methodology into a wall of text;
- use flowcharts, block diagrams, engineering diagrams, and clear captions;
- preserve logical and chronological flow;
- compress intelligently without making the method incomplete.

## 5. Results
Around 22:53–25:54:
- Results should be primarily visual: figures, charts, tables, prototype images, screenshots, and key numbers.
- Highlight performance / efficiency numbers when available.
- Compare against alternatives or baselines when possible.
- Use high-resolution images.
- Number figures/tables and add short captions.
- Avoid promotional or unsupported superlatives such as "best in the world".
- Scientific praise must be backed by measured results.

## 6. Innovation / originality
Around 25:54–27:16:
- Restate the actual point of originality clearly in its own section.
- Link the novelty directly to the application gap introduced earlier.
- Keep it short, usually two or three lines / a few points.
- A comparison table or other visual can be used to show differences from existing alternatives.
- Avoid empty claims such as "the newest" or "the best" without evidence.

## 7. Conclusion
Around 27:16–28:38:
- State what was actually achieved.
- Include the strongest measured numbers if appropriate.
- Explain the tangible impact on the target group, sector, or society.
- State the current readiness / maturity of the prototype or system.

## 8. Future work and references
Around 28:38–29:45:
- Future work must follow logically from current readiness.
- Use two or three concrete next steps.
- Make them technically and, where possible, temporally specific.
- Avoid grand claims such as "we will solve global pollution".
- Put references behind a dedicated QR code to save poster space.

## 9. Visual layout
Around 29:45–32:00:
- The poster is a visual product; first impression matters.
- Leave breathing room; do not fill every box to its edge.
- Methodology and Results may deserve more space than shorter text sections.
- Keep the official section order and template structure.
- Avoid tiny fonts, low-resolution visuals, and unnecessary text density.

## Diagnostic translation
These notes support an advisory "Judge Lens" for coaching, but must never be represented as an official SAIF scoring rubric.
