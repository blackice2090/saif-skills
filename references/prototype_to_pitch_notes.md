# From Prototype to Pitch

Based on the user-provided summary of Tuwaiq Academy's session, [From Prototype to Pitch: How to Present Your Results and Innovation to a Judging Panel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmvpJPNpxCk).

## Official Requirement / Official Guidance

Use `official_submission_rules.md` and current organizer instructions for the timing and permitted presentation materials. The session summary supplies expert advice and practice examples. It does not establish competition rules or judging weights.

## Expert Guidance

The speaker suggests five building blocks. Adapt them to the organizer's format and put the strongest supported result early enough for judges to follow the rest of the talk.

| Building block | What to explain |
| --- | --- |
| Opening and team | Start with a concrete problem or a sourced fact. Introduce members through work they actually did, and give each speaker a clear handoff. |
| Problem and opportunity | Name the people affected, describe what they struggle with today, and explain the gap the team saw. Give the source and context for any statistic. |
| Solution and innovation | Explain what was built and what it adds compared with the closest alternative. A different application, integration, or capability can be the contribution. Show real prototype images, recordings, or test artifacts when they help prove progress. |
| Impact and value | Say who benefits, what could improve, and how the team would measure it. Distinguish prototype results from adoption or impact that has yet to be measured. |
| Continued operation | Explain what the team will do after the competition. Identify the next pilot or validation step, who would operate the solution, and what would sustain it when relevant. Label prospective partners, funding, and expansion as plans until confirmed. |

Explain the value in plain language before going into implementation detail. Close with the strongest supported takeaway and the next action the team can realistically take. AI, a Vision 2030 connection, and a commercial business model are context-dependent suggestions. Use them when the project has a specific, defensible link.

## Training Example

- A 15-second pitch can name the beneficiary, problem, and solution. A 30-second version can add the differentiator or strongest result. A 60-second version can add test context, a limitation, or the next step. These are rehearsal formats.
- The session mentions three or four slides as one way to keep a prototype walkthrough concise. Use the presentation materials and format permitted for the actual session.

## Practical Coaching

- **Evidence check.** Map each headline claim to its source, metric or observation, and scope. When the team has only built a prototype, explain what it can demonstrate and what validation remains.
- **Timed run.** Give the entire pitch aloud, including handoffs and any demo. Cut background and repeated descriptions first. Keep the result, evidence, and meaningful limitation audible.
- **Fallback run.** Practice explaining the demo with a permitted backup, such as a screenshot or short recording, and rehearse a spoken version that works without a display.
- **Question drill.** Clarify an ambiguous question, give the direct answer first, then add evidence or a limitation as needed. Use `saif-jury-readiness` for a deeper challenge of claims or methods.
